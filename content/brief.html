What this means

  • Palantir is behind most of the growth. Its DoD contract obligations went from $138M in FY2020 to $1.33B in FY2026, 67% of the new entrants’ FY2026 total. Anduril also grew significantly, from $30M to $643M, almost entirely on fixed-price contracts.
  • Newer companies seem to be taking on more financial risk. 99.7% of Anduril’s FY2026 contract dollars are fixed-price, compared to 32–72% for the big five. This means Anduril generally has to cover unexpected costs. That said, the difference could also come from the types of projects these companies work on, like submarines and bombers versus software and drones.
  • What I’d check next. I’d look at Other Transaction agreements, since they aren’t fully captured in USAspending, so the newer companies’ totals could be higher. I’d also double-check the starting years, especially for Northrop: a $2.1B contract reduction in FY2020 makes its starting fixed-price share look higher, so its drop looks bigger than it really is.
