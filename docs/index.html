Fixed-price The price is set up front (firm-fixed, or with incentive or price-adjustment terms), so the contractor carries most of the cost risk. FPDS codes A, B, J, K, L, M.

Cost-reimbursement The government pays allowable costs, usually plus a fee (cost-plus), sometimes with no fee or shared costs, so the government carries most of the cost risk. FPDS codes R, S, T, U, V.

Scope: obligations (money committed on contracts), not payments or revenue. Other Transaction agreements are largely not in this data, which likely understates the new entrants.

What this means

  • Palantir is behind most of the growth. Its DoD contract obligations went from $138M in FY2020 to $1.33B in FY2026, 67% of the new entrants’ FY2026 total. Anduril also grew significantly, from $30M to $643M, almost entirely on fixed-price contracts.
  • Newer companies seem to be taking on more financial risk. 99.7% of Anduril’s FY2026 contract dollars are fixed-price, compared to 32–72% for the big five. This means Anduril generally has to cover unexpected costs. That said, the difference could also come from the types of projects these companies work on, like submarines and bombers versus software and drones.
  • What I’d check next. I’d look at Other Transaction agreements, since they aren’t fully captured in USAspending, so the newer companies’ totals could be higher. I’d also double-check the starting years, especially for Northrop: a $2.1B contract reduction in FY2020 makes its starting fixed-price share look higher, so its drop looks bigger than it really is.

Source & method

Net obligations on DoD-awarded contracts (award types A–D), grouped by transaction action date and fiscal year. Each of the 16 pricing codes is queried separately, all recipient pages are collected, and case-insensitive full-name regexes plus exact subsidiary lists select named legal entities. Fixed-price share is max(fixed-group net obligations, 0) divided by the sum of positive net obligations across configured pricing groups; a zero denominator is undefined.

6,262 recipient/code/year rows · 896 completed queries · pulled 2026-10-27.

90 company/name pairs were excluded by the matching rules; subsidiaries and joint ventures in this list remain attribution uncertainties.

Caveats

  • Other Transaction agreements (OTAs), a major route for new defense-tech awards, are largely not reported to USAspending and are outside award types A–D, so new entrants’ totals here likely understate their DoD business.
  • Obligations, not outlays; modifications can be negative.
  • Recipient matching is by name; subsidiaries outside the explicit list may be missed or mis-assigned.
  • FY2026 data may still be updating (fiscal year ended 2026-09-30).
  • Pricing type is recorded per transaction; large primes have mixed portfolios.
  • These are eight companies across twelve configured searches, not all defense spending; RTX searches Raytheon, RTX, Pratt & Whitney, Collins Aerospace, and Rockwell Collins.
  • Configured subsidiaries count in every FY regardless of acquisition date; joint ventures and uncertain names remain excluded.
  • This is not a complete corporate-family analysis; historical ownership and divestitures are not reconstructed.
  • Fixed-price share uses max(fixed-group net, 0) divided by the sum of positive net amounts across the 16 configured pricing codes; a zero denominator is undefined.
